Teaching Gemma 1B to count letters

“How many r's are in strawberry?” is the classic question small language models get wrong. Four thousand synthetic examples and a few minutes of fine-tuning take Google's smallest Gemma from 20% to 84% on words it has never seen.

Part of Teaching Gemma 1B, a series of small post-training experiments on one 1B model.

Base Gemma 3 1B counts letters correctly 20% of the time. Fine-tuned to spell the word out before counting, it gets 84% on words it never saw in training. Fine-tuned on the same questions with only the final number, it gets 49%.

The setup

Language models read tokens, not letters. To Gemma, “wellness” is one or two tokens, so “how many e's?” asks about something it never directly sees.

I generated 4,000 counting questions from a list of common English words, with the answers 0, 1, 2 and 3 equally common, and held out every fifth word for testing. Then I fine-tuned two models separately from the same checkpoint (gemma-3-1b-it) with identical settings. The only difference is the target response: one spells the word out and marks each match before answering, the other gives only the number.

The answer-only model is guessing

Given eight tries, the answer-only model gets 95% of questions right at least once, but only 49% on a single try. It learned which counts are plausible, not how to count. The spelled-out model gets 84% on the first try.

Strawberry

Base Gemma answers “How many r's are in strawberry?” correctly 16 times out of 16; it has almost certainly seen that exact question in training. The spelled-out model gets it right 0 times out of 16: it writes the double r as a single r and counts two. Its weak spot is spelling long words, not counting.

What this shows

  • SFT teaches procedures, not just answers. Both models saw the same questions and the same correct counts. Only the one shown the steps learned to count.
  • Small models fail at what their representation hides. Counting letters isn't hard; seeing them is. Writing the letters out first turns the question into one the model can answer.
