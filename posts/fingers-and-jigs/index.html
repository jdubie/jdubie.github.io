Doubling throughput with fingers and jigs

On a bimanual parallel-gripper robot, designing task-specific fingers and 3D-printed jigs doubled task throughput.

Generic parallel-jaw fingers and an unstructured work area make every grasp harder than it needs to be. Fingers shaped for the part, and jigs that hold it in a known place, take most of that difficulty out of the task. They also gave the wrist camera a much clearer view of the task, which matters as much for a learned policy as for the grasp itself.
