Teaching Gemma 1B Wordle: SFT for the rules, RL for the strategy

Starting from the raw pretrained checkpoint, which has never been taught to follow instructions.

Part of Teaching Gemma 1B, a series of small post-training experiments on one 1B model.

The pretrained Gemma 3 1B won 1 of 500 Wordle games. After supervised fine-tuning on 16,000 games where each move first writes out what is known so far, it wins 57%. Reinforcement learning on top of that is running now.

The plan

Wordle splits neatly into two skills. The rules: reply in the right format, guess real five-letter words, and only guess words that fit the colored feedback so far. The strategy: choose guesses that narrow the field fastest. Imitation is a good fit for the rules, since there is one right way to read the feedback. Reinforcement learning is a better fit for strategy, since the model can discover what works from its own games.

So SFT imitates a simple solver that guesses a random word consistent with everything seen so far. That solver plays correctly but not cleverly. SFT stops once the model wins roughly half its games, so that RL sees a mix of wins and losses to learn from.

Writing out the state helps a lot

Two SFT targets were compared. One is just the guess. The other first writes the current state, then the guess:

Pattern: _ A R _ _
Must include: A (not spot 3), R (not spot 2)
Ruled out: C E M T Y
Guess: PARKA

With the same 4,000 training games, the state-tracking version won twice as often (33% vs 16%). A 1B model has trouble keeping track of the feedback from earlier turns when it has to do so silently; writing it down makes each guess a much easier step.

RL: in progress

GRPO starts from the 16k SFT checkpoint: 32 answer words per step, 8 games each, reward for winning in fewer guesses, a small KL penalty to the SFT model. After 22 steps, win rate on training games has risen from about 70% to 82%, but held-out dev win rate has not moved (57.5% at step 0, 54.5% at step 20). That is too early to call; the run goes to 200 steps.
