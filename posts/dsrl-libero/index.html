Reproducing DSRL: latent-space RL on pi0, from sim to a real robot

This is a reproduction of Steering Your Diffusion Policy with Latent Space Reinforcement Learning (Wagenmaker et al., 2025), or DSRL, run on the authors' dsrl_pi0 code with a few changes of mine to connect it to our sim scene and our real arm. The method is theirs; what's new here is seeing whether it holds up on our own tasks.

Fine-tuning a big robot policy with RL is expensive and fragile. DSRL sidesteps that: it leaves pi0 frozen and trains a small RL policy that picks the noise pi0 starts its action sampling from. pi0 still produces every action; RL only chooses which of pi0's behaviours to ask for.

I reproduced it on two sim tasks first, a cube pick in our own MuJoCo scene and a LIBERO task, pick up the cream cheese and put it in the tray, then on a real arm.

Cube pick: 50% to about 95%

The first task is pick up the blue cube in a MuJoCo scene of our own, seen from the wrist camera. Base pi0 already got it half the time, so there was less to fix and more to prove: does the gain hold up over a long run?

The successful picks didn't get faster: about 100–110 steps before and after. Here RL bought reliability, not speed. It also never quite settled at 100%: late evaluations still dip to 60–70% now and then.

LIBERO: 3 of 10 to 10 of 10

Success dropped to 0–10% for the first 20k steps while the RL policy explored, then jumped to 100% by 40k and stayed at 80–100% after. The whole run took about 75 minutes.

PolicySuccessSteps to finish (median of successes)
Base pi03 / 10217
+ DSRL, 40k steps10 / 10165
+ DSRL, 190k steps10 / 10100

Here RL bought speed as well as reliability. Reliability came first; after that, with a discount of 0.999 and the episode capped at 400 steps, RL kept trimming time: the final policy finishes in less than half the steps of the base policy's successful attempts.

Real robot: tossing a candy bar

Then I tried it on a real YAM arm: pick up bar and toss into bin. pi0 was first fine-tuned on a small set of teleoperated demonstrations. DSRL then trained online on the robot itself, and I marked every trial a success or a failure by hand. I ran it for about an hour and a half, until the arm overheated.

The success rate over the last 100 trials went from 16% to 41%, with no sign of levelling off when the arm gave out. That's slower and noisier than in sim: every trial is a real toss, each one needs a reset, and the rate here includes the exploration the RL policy does while it learns.

There's no video. The trials went straight into the RL policy's in-memory replay buffer and were never saved, which is the first thing I'd change next time.

Some gotchas along the way

The paper describes π0's noise as a 1,600-dimensional action space: a chunk of 50 actions × 32 dimensions. The released code doesn't do that. SAC outputs 32 numbers, and they're repeated across the whole chunk. I asked about it on GitHub, and others in that thread have since tried interpolating and shuffling the noise instead.

The reason is SAC's curse of dimensionality: soft actor-critic gets much harder to train as the action space grows, so the method leans on repeating a small choice into pi0's 32 × chunk-size noise. I tried a few ways to give SAC more dimensions to work with, on LIBERO:

How SAC's output becomes pi0's noiseRunsBest success
Repeat 32 values across the chunk (the released code)3290–100%
Repeat, after mapping SAC's squashed output back to a Gaussian (arctanh)7100% (the run above)
One value per noise dimension, all 1,600, with arctanh610%
Repeat, plus a random noise schedule1120–30%

Giving SAC more dimensions only made it worse. These are quick runs, not a careful ablation: one seed each, and many were stopped early.

Setup

  • In sim, soft actor-critic picks a new noise vector every 20 control steps on LIBERO and every 50 on the cube pick, from one 64×64 image plus the robot state.
  • Ten Q-functions, batch 256, and a reward of 1 only on success.
  • pi0 is never updated. The RL policy is a small CNN and MLP.
